# E071 — computed deck-balance context for Jev macro decisions

Issue: [#134](https://github.com/yzxoi/RSI-Jev-Slay-the-Spire-2/issues/134). Baseline: merged `main` at `0ab503b2a225d2aac1c031d4d13bff4a4b9d5253`. E065 and E068 both ended as saved Ironclad A0 floor-33 defeats, and E069's repaired headless sample had 0/24 wins. E068's late deck had strong attack/Strength lines and limited reliable block/draw. These observations motivate a deck-computation experiment; they do not prove a card-reward error caused either loss.

Hypothesis: Jev can make stronger reward and shop choices if its existing full deck input is accompanied by a deterministic compact profile: deck size; Attack/Skill/Power counts; counts of cards whose exported printed stats include block, draw or energy; and starter Strike/Defend counts. Give a short advisory to consider reliable defense/draw when attack-heavy, without removing legal candidates or forcing a selection. The baseline is the same Jev macro policy without the computed context. Both arms use identical `retaliate` combat planning.

Fixed evaluation before implementation: pinned sts2-cli `084d1aa3d8e118ca7ce8d8774ad16d6be9c92367`, headless game v0.111.0, Jev `typesafe/jev-1.13-20260917`; fresh seeds `e071_balance_001` through `_004`, Ironclad and Silent, A0 and A10, policies `retaliate` and `retaliate_deck_context` (16 pairs/32 episodes). Cap each episode at 1500 actions/180 seconds, run four workers, and cap shared Jev at 5000 calls/$1.50. Record every status, act/floor/HP, victory, deck-profile exposure count, cost, exact code/dependency hashes and verified ignored raw trace hashes. Freeze arithmetic and missing-field controls in unit tests. No HP, reward, RNG or victory edits.

Decision: promote only if all episodes finish without a new execution error, treatment exposes at least eight reward/shop decisions, at least four pairs improve act/floor depth with at most two regressions, and no observed baseline victory is lost. Otherwise close as negative/inconclusive and preserve traces. Even a passing 16-pair result would require replication because Jev calls and game trajectories are stochastic. Native strength is separate.

Implementation at exact tested SHA `8236713528b3099447f0afebdaa09b409f9fd205` added a deterministic `deck_profile` only to treatment Jev contexts for card rewards and shops. It counts exported printed stats and offers an advisory when Attack count is at least twice printed-block-card count in a deck of at least 15; it neither filters candidates nor changes combat planning. `python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -q` passed 115 tests, including arithmetic, missing-field and no-mutation controls. No native controller behavior changed.

The frozen batch command `python3 -m scripts.evaluate_deck_e071` completed 32/32 episodes. All were normal defeats, with zero wins, stalls or execution errors. All 32 ignored raw trace SHA-256 values were verified and the exact code/dependency/game hashes, all configs and every outcome are in [`result.json`](result.json). The treatment delivered 166 profile contexts at reward/shop decisions. Of 16 act/floor pairs, **two** treatment runs went deeper, **three** went less deep, and 11 tied; the predeclared promotion threshold of four improvements and at most two regressions failed. The shared budget logged 983 Jev calls, $0.170566494 provider-reported cost, $0.017472 estimated for unavailable usage and 13 uncertain model calls, so the total is not fully provider-reported.

Trace inspection gives concrete possible failure modes, not causal proof. In Ironclad A0 seed `_003`, both arms made identical reward choices through floor 3, but at the floor-4 shop treatment bought Drum of Battle where baseline bought Perfected Strike as its second card; treatment died on act 1 floor 9 versus baseline floor 15. In Silent A0 seed `_004`, the treatment began choosing Footwork and Deflect where the baseline took Hidden Daggers and Phantom Blades, and died at the act-1 boss versus baseline act 2 floor 3. Those examples are consistent with over-weighting defense before the early damage check. Conversely, Ironclad A10 seed `_001` took the same listed reward/shop actions through floor 5 but treatment died floor 9 and baseline floor 17, showing that policy attribution cannot rely on card choices alone: route/combat stochasticity or other divergent decisions can matter. Headless paired seeds are exploratory, not deterministic causal counterfactuals.

Decision: close the PR without merging the profile policy. It passed arithmetic and execution compatibility but missed the preregistered strength criterion and produced no wins. Raw local traces and compact negative results remain accessible on the closed PR branch. A follow-up should model imminent encounter damage requirements and card effects more precisely, then test tactical and macro changes separately; merely counting card types is too coarse for promotion.
